How a grade is setfixed by rule, written before any result

A · 85-100 A validated desk pick: the edge over DraftKings is outside noise and held up out of sample.

B · 65-80 A forward-test desk pick: the model beats the DraftKings close this season, not yet outside noise.

Within A and B, the score is risk-adjusted: expected profit per unit of risk (EV divided by the standard deviation of the bet's result). A +440 underdog with a 17% edge swings far more than a −440 favorite with a 9% edge, and per unit of risk the favorite is the better bet, so it scores higher. Desk picks can be underdogs; leans cannot.

C · 40-60 A model lean: the favourite our model and DraftKings both favour, each at least 55%, priced no shorter than −200 (so a lean pays at least half a unit). It is not an edge; over time a lean is expected to lose about the book's margin. A lean is always a favorite by design: an underdog lean would mean betting against both our model and the market, and this season every way of betting our model against DraftKings has lost. At DraftKings' own probability a lean's risk-adjusted value is negative, so it is scored on how strongly model and market agree, not on reward.

D No bet. The model's side is shown for information only. When the model disagrees with DraftKings, betting the model has lost this season.

HOLD No side while the desk waits on a quarterback or a day-to-day player.

★ Alpha picks are the three to five highest-scored bets of the day, whatever their grade, locked at 11:00 AM ET so the list does not reshuffle. On a day with no A or B bet they are grade C leans, and the card says so. They are separate from the MLB Alpha Pick record, which has its own edge gate.

Line moves and hold. Each card shows where the moneyline opened (the first DraftKings price we recorded), how many points of win probability it has moved since, and DraftKings' hold, the margin built into the two prices. A big move toward a side usually means informed money; a high hold means a worse price for either side.

Closing line value (CLV) is how sharps judge a bet before the result is known: did we get a better price than DraftKings' last price before the game started? "+2.0 pts" means our price implied a 2-point lower win probability than the close, so we got the better number. Over a few dozen bets, CLV says far more than the win-loss record. A lean taken at the market price should average about zero.

Only A, B and C are bets. They are logged at the DraftKings price shown and graded on the final score, outside the curated record. Prices are DraftKings moneylines read from ESPN's scoreboard feed, each with the time it was observed.
