A true arb pays a small but guaranteed profit. The "Stake Split" tells you the optimal % to put on each side so payouts equalize. Real-world snag: books cap bet size and ban frequent arbers. Use these sparingly, mix in -EV plays to look like a normal bettor.

◆ MIDDLErisk = juice paid, payoff = double win if game lands in the window
MarketTypeSide ASide BMiddle Window

A middle is when you can bet OVER at one line and UNDER at another. If the game total lands BETWEEN them, both bets win — a "middle hit." You risk a small loss (the combined juice) and win a payoff if it middles. In MLB, totals middles cash ~7% of the time historically, which is enough to make zero-juice middles +EV.

▾ LOW VIG (under 3%)cheapest 2-way pairings across books
MarketSide ASide BCombined Vig

Standard markets carry 4-5% vig. By splitting one side at book A and the other at book B, you can routinely find pairings with under 3% combined vig — meaning your model only needs a small edge to clear it. Pinnacle is usually one side of every low-vig pair.

🎓 The reality check

Arb betting looks too good to be true on paper. In practice:

  • Limits matter. A $50 arb is real money in volume, but most books will cap your stake at $200-$1,000 within a few weeks of betting only arbs.
  • Books talk to each other. A line move on Pinnacle triggers immediate moves on others. Arb windows often last 30-90 seconds before they close.
  • Bonuses change the math. When books offer "bet $200, get $200" promos, the +EV math on arbs becomes massive temporarily. Hunt these.
  • Pinnacle is your friend. They have the sharpest book and accept arbers. Most arb sides should be there.
  • Mix in action. If 100% of your bets are arbs, you'll be banned. Sprinkle in -EV parlays so you look square. Cost of doing business.
